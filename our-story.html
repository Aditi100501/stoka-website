Our Beginning

How an arts teacher saw a gap -- and decided to fill it.

A journey of rhythm, inclusion, and the simple belief that every body deserves the chance to move with joy.

The Gap

During her years as an arts teacher, Surbhi watched something remarkable happen every time music started playing. Students who struggled to express themselves through words would suddenly find their voices through rhythm. They found confidence in the tilt of a head or the stretch of an arm.

But she also noticed a heartbreaking disparity. For children with physical and developmental disabilities, the world of dance was often gated. High-energy studios and rigid techniques didn't account for the unique, beautiful ways these children moved. The gap wasn't just in access; it was in understanding that dance isn't about perfection—it's about expression.

The 2019-2020 sessions were transformative. We saw students who had never participated in physical education finding rhythm and community. These moments were captured through the sensitive lens of photographer and filmmaker Isha Thorat, whose work immortalized the pure joy of these early classes.

"The movement wasn't just physical; it was emotional liberation." — Class Participant

2020: The Halt

Then Covid came

In March 2020, our rooms went silent. The momentum we had built was suddenly halted by a global pandemic that disproportionately affected the very families we served. Classes were paused, and for a long time, the music was quiet.

We are back

The heartbeat of Stoka never stopped. After years of reflection and rebuilding, we are thrilled to announce our relaunch in 2026. We are returning with new energy, expanded programs, and the same radical inclusive warmth.

The Founder

Surbhi

At 24, Surbhi is not just a teacher; she's a visionary who believes that physical barriers are simply puzzles waiting to be solved. With a background in classical dance and special education, she brings a unique hybrid perspective to every workshop.

"My vision for Stoka is a world where no child looks at a dance floor and thinks, 'That’s not for me.' We are rewriting the choreography of inclusion, one step, one wheel, one heartbeat at a time."
