The Access Gap in the Arts

In many parts of India, dance and the arts are viewed through a narrow lens of performance or tradition. For children with diverse abilities, the doors to these spaces are often closed—not just by physical barriers, but by a deep-seated social stigma.

We see a lack of inclusive programs that recognize movement as a fundamental human right. Art shouldn't be a privilege of the neurotypical or the physically able; it should be the common ground where we all meet to discover who we are.

The Hidden Barrier

Stigma is often a quiet, pervasive doubt. It’s the look of pity from a passerby or the low expectations set by society. For Stoka’s founder, this wasn’t just a theoretical problem—it was an lived observation.

"I saw that the biggest obstacle wasn't the physical or cognitive diagnosis. It was the collective assumption that these individuals couldn't dance, couldn't create, or didn't need to express themselves artistically. Breaking that stigma is our first step in every session."

By creating a space where the "perfection" of a movement is measured by its authenticity rather than its form, we are dismantling the barriers of doubt one heartbeat at a time.
